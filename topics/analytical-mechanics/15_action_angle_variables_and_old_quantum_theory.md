# Module 15: Action-angle variables and the old quantum theory (★★)

**Books:** Goldstein 10.6 (action-angle variables, one degree of freedom), 10.7 (completely separable systems: skim), 10.8 (the Kepler problem in action-angle variables); Landau and Lifshitz *Mechanics* sections 49 to 52 (optional); Griffiths *Quantum Mechanics* (WKB chapter) for the quantum counterpart; your Zettili guide, Section 9.4 (WKB, p. 566).
**Time:** 2 weeks (four sessions).

## Why this matters for the bridge

The **action variable** $J=\oint p\,dq$ (the phase-space area enclosed by one period) is the same integral you computed in Module 07. **The old quantum theory quantized exactly this object**: $\oint p\,dq=nh$ (Bohr-Sommerfeld). That condition is precisely the leading-order WKB quantization condition, with a correction (the Maslov index) that supplies the zero-point $\frac12$. So the historical route into quantum mechanics ran straight through this section, and the modern semiclassical approximation lands back on it. It also shows *why* the classical quantity that is quantized is the action: $J$ is an adiabatic invariant (Module 20) and the canonical conjugate of the angle variable.

## Skip-test (40 minutes, no notes)

1. Define $J=\oint p\,dq$ and compute it for the harmonic oscillator: $J=2\pi E/\omega$; invert to $E(J)=\frac{\omega J}{2\pi}$.
2. Define the angle variable $w$ and the frequency $\nu=\frac{\partial H}{\partial J}$ (in terms of $\nu=\omega/2\pi$); show $w=\nu t+w_0$.
3. Apply $\oint p\,dq=nh$ to the harmonic oscillator and to a particle in a box and compare with the exact quantum levels.
4. State the WKB quantization condition $\oint p\,dq=(n+\frac12)h$ for a well with two smooth turning points, and the modifications for a hard wall.
5. Show for the Kepler problem that $E$ depends only on $J_r+J_\theta+J_\phi$.

## Detailed topics (checklist)

**Action-angle variables for one degree of freedom (10.6) [Must]**
- For a periodic (librating or rotating) motion the canonical transformation $(q,p)\to(w,J)$ with $J=\oint p\,dq$ (in some books with a factor $1/2\pi$) makes $H=H(J)$ only; $w=\nu t+w_0$ with $\nu=\frac{\partial H}{\partial J}$; $J$ is the phase-space area enclosed by the orbit; $w$ increases by 1 per period. The generating function is $W(q,J)=\int p\,dq$.
- Harmonic oscillator: $J=2\pi E/\omega$, $E=\frac{\omega J}{2\pi}$, $\nu=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}$ (independent of energy: the frequency does not depend on the amplitude).
- Pendulum: $\nu$ depends on $J$ (anharmonic), decreasing with amplitude.

**Completely separable systems (10.7) [Could]**
- $J_i=\oint p_i\,dq_i$ for each separated coordinate; the energy $H(J_1,\dots,J_n)$; **degeneracy** when frequencies are commensurate or when $H$ depends only on a combination of the $J_i$; **integrability** (Liouville-Arnold) means $n$ independent conserved quantities in involution, with motion on tori.

**The Kepler problem (10.8) [Must]**
- $J_\phi=2\pi L_z$, $J_\theta+J_\phi=2\pi L$, $J_r=-2\pi L+\frac{2\pi\mu k}{\sqrt{-2\mu E}}$; hence $E=-\frac{2\pi^2\mu k^2}{(J_r+J_\theta+J_\phi)^2}$: $E$ depends on the *sum* only, the classical origin of the degeneracy (closed orbits); with $J_i=n_ih$ this gives the Bohr levels $E_n=-\frac{\mu k^2}{2\hbar^2n^2}$, $n=n_r+n_\theta+n_\phi$ (Module 05: the $SO(4)$ symmetry).

**The old quantum theory and WKB [Must]**
- Bohr-Sommerfeld: $\oint p_i\,dq_i=n_ih$; smooth turning points give the correction $n\to n+\frac12$ (Maslov index $\mu=2$ for two turning points): $\oint p\,dq=(n+\frac12)h$; a hard wall adds a phase $\pi$ (index 2) each, giving $\oint p\,dq=nh$ with $n=1,2,\dots$ for a box; one smooth turning point plus one hard wall gives $n-\frac14$ with $n=1,2,\dots$ (bouncing ball).
- The WKB wavefunction $\psi\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}e^{\pm\frac i\hbar\int p\,dq}$ (Module 16).

## Derive from scratch (A sessions, book closed)

1. $J=\oint p\,dq$ and $w=\nu t+w_0$ from the canonical transformation generated by $W(q,J)=\int p\,dq$.
2. $E(J)$ for the harmonic oscillator, and $\nu=\partial E/\partial J$.
3. $E(J_r,J_\theta,J_\phi)$ for the Kepler problem (the radial integral).
4. The Bohr levels from $J=nh$.
5. The Maslov-corrected harmonic-oscillator spectrum $E_n=(n+\frac12)\hbar\omega$.

## Bare-minimum exercises (with expected results)

1. **Harmonic oscillator:** $J=\frac{2\pi E}{\omega}$; impose $J=nh$ to get $E_n=n\hbar\omega$; with the Maslov correction $E_n=(n+\frac12)\hbar\omega$, the exact quantum result. Where did the zero-point energy go in the old theory? (Into the Maslov index.)
2. **Box:** $J=2L\sqrt{2mE}=nh$ gives $E_n=\frac{n^2h^2}{8mL^2}$, $n=1,2,\dots$, the exact quantum result.
3. **Bouncing ball** ($V=Fx$ for $x>0$, hard wall at $x=0$): derive $\oint p\,dq=2\int_0^{E/F}\sqrt{2m(E-Fx)}\,dx=\frac{4\sqrt{2m}\,E^{3/2}}{3F}$ and impose $\oint p\,dq=(n-\frac14)h$ to get $E_n=\left(\frac{\hbar^2F^2}{2m}\right)^{1/3}\left[\frac{3\pi}{2}(n-\tfrac14)\right]^{2/3}$. Compare with the exact Airy-function values $|a_n|=2.338,4.088,5.521$ (in units of $(\hbar^2F^2/2m)^{1/3}$): the WKB values are $2.320,4.082,5.517$, errors below 1%.
4. **Angle variable:** for the oscillator show $q=\sqrt{\frac{J}{\pi m\omega}}\sin2\pi w$, $p=\sqrt{\frac{J m\omega}{\pi}}\cos2\pi w$ and that $\{w,J\}=1$ (the transformation is canonical).
5. **Pendulum:** compute $J(E)$ numerically (elliptic integral) for $\theta_0=10^\circ,60^\circ,120^\circ$ and the frequency $\nu=\partial E/\partial J$; verify $\nu$ decreases with amplitude and vanishes at the separatrix.
6. **Isotropic 2D oscillator:** $E=\frac{\omega}{2\pi}(J_x+J_y)$ so the degeneracy of level $n=n_x+n_y$ is $n+1$ (compare with the quantum spectrum $E=\hbar\omega(n+1)$ with the Maslov correction per dimension).
7. **Kepler:** show $J_r=2\pi\left(\frac{\mu k}{\sqrt{-2\mu E}}-L\right)$ (the radial integral between turning points) and deduce $E=-\frac{2\pi^2\mu k^2}{(J_r+J_\theta+J_\phi)^2}$; with $J_i=n_ih$ (and $k=e^2$ in Gaussian units) recover $E_n=-13.6\,{\rm eV}/n^2$ and the Bohr radius $a_0=\hbar^2/(\mu e^2)$.
8. **Adiabatic invariance preview:** if $\omega$ varies slowly, $J=\frac{2\pi E}{\omega}$ is constant, so $E/\omega$ is constant: a pendulum whose string is slowly shortened has amplitude $\theta_0\propto\ell^{-3/4}$ (derive using $E=\frac12mg\ell\theta_0^2$).
9. **WKB wavefunction:** show that $\psi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(x)}}e^{\frac i\hbar\int p\,dx}$ solves the Schrödinger equation to leading order in $\hbar$ when $|p'|\hbar\ll p^2$ (substitute and expand).
10. **Quantization of a non-standard well:** for $V=\lambda|x|$ use $\oint p\,dq=(n+\frac12)h$ ($n=0,1,\dots$) to estimate the lowest levels and check scaling $E_n\propto n^{2/3}$ (compare with the bouncing-ball scaling).

## Optional extension

Show that Bohr-Sommerfeld is exact for the harmonic oscillator (with the Maslov correction) and for the Coulomb problem (a numerical coincidence connected to the $SO(4)$ symmetry), but fails for generic anharmonic potentials at $O(\hbar^2)$.

## Pitfalls

- Forgetting that $J$ is an area, with factors of $2\pi$ varying between books.
- Omitting the Maslov correction and concluding that the oscillator has $E=n\hbar\omega$.
- Applying WKB near a turning point (it fails there; use connection formulas).
- Believing $J$ is quantized because of any deep classical reason: it is a heuristic that works because $J$ is the adiabatic invariant.

## Gate questions

1. Why is the action variable the natural quantity to quantize?
2. Where does the zero-point energy come from in the semiclassical picture?
3. Why does the Kepler energy depend on a single combination of the actions?
