# Module 10: Generators, symmetry groups and Liouville's theorem (★★★)

**Books:** Goldstein 9.5 to 9.8 (section titles vary between printings: the content you want is (a) $\dot u=\{u,H\}$, (b) infinitesimal canonical transformations, (c) generators of symmetries, (d) the angular-momentum algebra, (e) symmetry groups, (f) Liouville); Sakurai *Modern Quantum Mechanics* 1.6 to 1.7 (read the week after this module); José and Saletan chapters 5 to 6.
**Time:** 3 weeks (six sessions). **The most important module of the guide.**

## Why this matters for the bridge

This is the module where the classical-quantum transition stops being blurry. The results, in the order they should land:

1. **$\dot u=\{u,H\}+\partial_tu$.** Time evolution *is* a Poisson bracket with $H$. Compare $i\hbar\,\dot{\hat u}=[\hat u,\hat H]$, the Heisenberg equation. Same statement.
2. **An infinitesimal canonical transformation generated by $G$ shifts any observable by $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$.** $G=H$ generates time translation; $G=p_x$ generates spatial translation; $G=L_z$ generates rotation about $z$. **The conserved quantities are the generators of the symmetries that conserve them.** This dissolves the question of why momentum "is" $-i\hbar\partial_x$ in QM: it is not a definition pulled from a hat; it is the generator of translations, exactly as in classical mechanics.
3. **$\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$**, the classical angular-momentum algebra, with the same structure as $[\hat L_i,\hat L_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat L_k$.
4. **A quantity is conserved iff it Poisson-commutes with $H$ iff it generates a symmetry of $H$.** Three statements, one fact.
5. **Liouville's theorem** (phase-space volume is preserved) is the classical ancestor of unitarity.

## Skip-test (40 minutes, no notes)

1. Show that the infinitesimal canonical transformation generated by $G$ is $\delta q_i=\epsilon\frac{\partial G}{\partial p_i}$, $\delta p_i=-\epsilon\frac{\partial G}{\partial q_i}$, equivalently $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$.
2. Show that $p_x$ generates translations, $L_z$ generates rotations (with their action on both $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf p$), and $H$ generates time evolution.
3. Show that if $\{G,H\}=0$ then $G$ is conserved *and* $H$ is invariant under the transformation $G$ generates (one theorem, not two).
4. Derive the Lie-series form of a finite transformation $u(\epsilon)=\sum_n\frac{\epsilon^n}{n!}\{\dots\{u,G\},\dots,G\}$.
5. State and prove Liouville's theorem (the divergence of the Hamiltonian flow in phase space is zero) and give the equation $\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\{\rho,H\}=0$.

## Detailed topics (checklist)

**Equations of motion in bracket form and infinitesimal canonical transformations (9.5) [Must]**
- $\dot u=\{u,H\}+\partial_tu$; infinitesimal canonical transformation $F_2=\sum q_iP_i+\epsilon G(q,P)$ gives $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$; the **generator** $G$ and the transformation it generates; $G=H$ and $\epsilon=dt$ gives the motion itself (the flow of a Hamiltonian is a one-parameter group of canonical transformations).
- **Symmetry and conservation as one statement:** $\{G,H\}=0\Leftrightarrow$ $G$ conserved $\Leftrightarrow$ $H$ invariant under the flow of $G$ (using the antisymmetry of the bracket: $\delta_GH=\epsilon\{H,G\}=-\epsilon\{G,H\}$).
- **Finite transformations:** exponentiation $u(\epsilon)=e^{\epsilon\{\cdot,G\}}u=u+\epsilon\{u,G\}+\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\{\{u,G\},G\}+\dots$ (Lie series).

**Angular-momentum brackets (9.6) [Must]**
- $\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$; $\{L^2,L_i\}=0$; $\{L_i,V_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}V_k$ for vector observables; $L_z$ generates rotations of both coordinates and momenta.

**Symmetry groups of mechanical systems (9.7) [Must]**
- The generators of a symmetry group close under the Poisson bracket into a **Lie algebra** (by the Jacobi identity: the bracket of two conserved quantities is conserved); the finite transformations form a **Lie group**. Examples: translations and rotations (the Euclidean algebra), the Galilean algebra with generators $H,\mathbf P,\mathbf L,\mathbf K=m\mathbf r-\mathbf Pt$; the **central extension** $\{K_i,P_j\}=m\delta_{ij}$ (the mass appears where one expects zero).
- This is the classical shadow of representation theory in QM: the quantum states carry representations of the same algebra.

**Liouville's theorem (9.8) [Must]**
- Phase-space flow is incompressible: $\sum_i\left(\frac{\partial\dot q_i}{\partial q_i}+\frac{\partial\dot p_i}{\partial p_i}\right)=0$; the density along trajectories is constant; $\{\rho,H\}+\partial_t\rho=0$; consequences: no attractors in Hamiltonian flow, Poincaré recurrence (statement). **Quantum analogue:** unitarity (the flow preserves the inner product); the density matrix obeys $i\hbar\dot\rho=[H,\rho]$, the quantum Liouville (von Neumann) equation.
- Connection to statistical mechanics: ensembles evolve incompressibly in phase space (Thermodynamics guide, ensemble modules).

## Derive from scratch (A sessions, book closed)

1. $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$ from the generating function $F_2=qP+\epsilon G$.
2. The translation, rotation and time-evolution generators and their action on $(\mathbf r,\mathbf p)$.
3. The equivalence "$G$ conserved $\Leftrightarrow\{G,H\}=0\Leftrightarrow G$ generates a symmetry of $H$".
4. The Lie-series formula for finite transformations.
5. Liouville's theorem via the divergence of the flow.

## Bare-minimum exercises (with expected results)

1. **Translations:** for $G=p_x$ compute $\delta x=\epsilon\{x,p_x\}=\epsilon$, $\delta p=0$.
2. **Rotations:** for $G=L_z=xp_y-yp_x$ show $\delta x=-\epsilon y$, $\delta y=\epsilon x$ (a counterclockwise rotation by $\epsilon$), $\delta p_x=-\epsilon p_y$, $\delta p_y=\epsilon p_x$: both position and momentum rotate.
3. **Time evolution:** $G=H$, $\epsilon=dt$: $\delta u=dt\{u,H\}$; for $H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2q^2$ check $\dot q=p/m$, $\dot p=-m\omega^2q$.
4. **Finite rotation (Lie series):** exponentiate $L_z$ on $x$: $x(\phi)=x\cos\phi-y\sin\phi$, $y(\phi)=x\sin\phi+y\cos\phi$ (sum the series).
5. **Conservation and symmetry:** show that if $V$ depends on $x$ only then $\{L_z,H\}\ne0$ and $L_z$ is not conserved; if $V=V(r)$ then $\{L_z,H\}=0$ and the Hamiltonian is invariant under rotations about $z$.
6. **Galilean algebra:** with $\mathbf K=m\mathbf r-\mathbf Pt$ for a free particle compute $\{K_i,P_j\}=m\delta_{ij}$, $\{K_i,K_j\}=0$, $\{L_i,K_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}K_k$, and show $\frac{dK_i}{dt}=\frac{\partial K_i}{\partial t}+\{K_i,H\}=0$.
7. **Euclidean algebra in the plane:** with $P_x,P_y,L_z$ show $\{L_z,P_x\}=P_y$, $\{L_z,P_y\}=-P_x$, $\{P_x,P_y\}=0$; interpret: translations form an abelian ideal and rotations act on it.
8. **Liouville:** show that the flow of any $H(q,p)$ has zero divergence in phase space; contrast with the damped oscillator $\dot p=-m\omega^2q-\gamma p$, whose phase-space area shrinks as $e^{-\gamma t}$ (non-Hamiltonian flow).
9. **Evolving ensemble:** an initial disc in the $(q,p)$ plane for the harmonic oscillator rotates rigidly; for the pendulum the same disc is sheared into a thin filament while its area is constant.
10. **Dilation (optional):** for $V=\frac\alpha{r^2}$ show $D=\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf p-2Ht$ is conserved: $\frac{dD}{dt}=\{D,H\}+\partial_tD=0$; identify the transformation $D$ generates (scaling).

## Optional extension

Quantize the structure: replace $\{,\}\to\frac{1}{i\hbar}[,]$ and show that $\hat U(\epsilon)=e^{-i\epsilon\hat G/\hbar}$ implements $\delta\hat u=\epsilon\frac{1}{i\hbar}[\hat u,\hat G]$ to first order. Then read Sakurai 1.6 to 1.7 and compare line by line with Exercises 1 and 2.

## Pitfalls

- Treating "conserved quantity" and "generator of a symmetry" as two facts.
- Sign errors in $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$ (check with $G=p_x$: $\delta x=+\epsilon$).
- Forgetting that the generator acts on *every* phase-space function, including momenta.
- Believing the Poisson bracket of two generators is always a generator without checking the Jacobi identity (it is, but the algebra can acquire central terms).

## Gate questions

1. Why is "conserved" the same as "generates a symmetry"?
2. What does the central term $\{K_i,P_j\}=m\delta_{ij}$ mean, and why is it significant?
3. How is Liouville's theorem the classical ancestor of unitarity?
