# Module 21: Synthesis, mini-projects and mock exam

**Books:** all, plus your three written Bridge documents.
**Time:** 3 weeks (six sessions): one for the blank-page gate, two for the synthesis document, three for the mini-projects.

## Goal

Decide with evidence whether the classical-quantum transition is no longer blurry: that you can reproduce the key derivations from a blank page, that you can state precisely what is derived and what is assumed in each bridge, and that the three Bridge documents are merged into one narrative. The artifact this guide exists to produce is `classical-quantum-bridge.md`.

## Part 1: the blank-page gate (no notes, 15 to 25 minutes each)

Mark each when you can do it cleanly. Do three per session, in a different order each time.

1. Legendre transformation $(q,\dot q)\to(q,p)$; state what structure phase space has that velocity space does not.
2. Define a canonical transformation in three equivalent ways: form-preserving, symplectic ($MJM^T=J$), preserving the fundamental brackets.
3. Show that an infinitesimal canonical transformation generated by $G$ gives $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$; identify the generators of translation, rotation and time evolution.
4. Derive $\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$; write the quantum counterpart; state what is and is not derived in passing between them (Dirac's argument and Groenewold-van Hove).
5. Write the Hamilton-Jacobi equation; explain what $S$ is and what $\partial S/\partial q$ means.
6. Substitute $\psi=Ae^{iS/\hbar}$ into the Schrödinger equation; recover HJ plus continuity as $\hbar\to0$; identify the residual term.
7. Define $J=\oint p\,dq$; recover the harmonic-oscillator levels from $J=nh$; state where the zero-point energy went.
8. Take a discrete oscillator chain to the continuum; obtain a field Lagrangian density and the wave equation; state the field Poisson bracket.
9. State and prove Noether's theorem for an internal symmetry; identify the conserved current for a complex scalar.
10. Reproduce the master table of Module 19 from memory.

**Passing criterion:** at least 8 of 10 done cleanly, and the remaining two understood after one reading. Items 4, 6 and 9 are not negotiable.

## Part 2: the synthesis deliverable, `classical-quantum-bridge.md` (10 to 15 pages)

Merge `bridge1.md`, `bridge2.md` and `bridge3.md` into a single narrative with this structure:

1. **The question** (one page): what the blurriness consisted of; the four confusions of the overview and the fifth, real one (Groenewold-van Hove).
2. **The algebraic bridge** (three pages): Poisson bracket $\to$ commutator; generators; the angular-momentum algebra; what is derived and what is assumed.
3. **The analytic bridge** (four pages): HJ, $\psi=Ae^{iS/\hbar}$, stationary phase, the path integral; the singular classical limit.
4. **The field bridge** (three pages): chain $\to$ continuum; field brackets; Noether; gauge invariance and minimal coupling.
5. **The master table** and **a closing page** on where each bridge fails (ordering ambiguity, singular limit, anomalies) and why the arrow points from quantum to classical.

**Rubric (10 points each):** correctness of each bridge; honesty about what is assumed; the master table; clarity (someone who knows only Griffiths should follow it); a closing position on what "quantization" is.

## Part 3: four mini-projects (Python, standalone, 100 to 250 lines each)

### Project 1: symplectic integrators versus RK4
- **Task:** integrate the pendulum and a Kepler orbit ($e=0.5$) with leapfrog and with RK4 for $10^3$ to $10^5$ periods.
- **Steps:** (1) plot energy error versus time for both; (2) compute the one-step Jacobian by finite differences and check $\det M=1$ and $MJM^T=J$; (3) show that RK4's Kepler orbit spuriously precesses and its energy drifts, while leapfrog's orbit closes on a slightly modified Hamiltonian; (4) evolve a disc of initial conditions in the pendulum phase plane and measure its area over time.
- **Checks:** leapfrog energy error stays bounded $O(\Delta t^2)$; $\det M_{\rm leapfrog}=1$ to rounding; RK4's energy error grows with time; area of the evolving blob constant to the accuracy of the area estimate.

### Project 2: LRL vector and perihelion precession
- **Task:** integrate a Kepler orbit with a small perturbation and track $\mathbf A$.
- **Steps:** (1) pure $1/r$: verify $\mathbf A$ is constant to rounding; (2) add $-\beta/r^2$ and measure the precession per orbit against $\Delta\phi=2\pi\mu\beta/L^2$; (3) add a $1/r^3$ term to mimic general relativity and check the perihelion advance $\Delta\phi=\frac{6\pi GM}{c^2a(1-e^2)}$; for Mercury, $5.0\times10^{-7}$ rad per orbit, i.e. $43''$ per century.
- **Checks:** the numerical precession within a few percent of the analytic rates; $\mathbf A$ rotates uniformly in the perturbed case.

### Project 3: chain to continuum
- **Task:** normal modes of a loaded string and its continuum limit.
- **Steps:** (1) build $M$ and $K$ for $N=3,10,100$ masses and diagonalize with `numpy.linalg.eigh`; compare $\omega_n=2\sqrt{k/m}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2(N+1)}$; (2) plot the dispersion relation and the $\omega=ck$ line; (3) propagate a Gaussian pulse and measure its speed ($c=a\sqrt{k/m}$) and its dispersion at short wavelengths; (4) add the zero-point energy $\frac12\sum\omega_n$ and watch it grow with $N$.
- **Checks:** eigenfrequencies agree to rounding; pulse speed within a percent of $c$; zero-point sum $\propto N^2$.

### Project 4: Bohr-Sommerfeld/WKB versus the Schrödinger equation
- **Task:** quantize $H=\frac{p^2}{2}+x^4$ ($\hbar=m=1$) semiclassically and compare with a numerical solution.
- **Steps:** (1) compute $\oint p\,dq=4\sqrt2\,E^{3/4}\int_0^1\sqrt{1-u^4}\,du=4.944\,E^{3/4}$ and impose $=2\pi(n+\frac12)$; (2) solve the Schrödinger equation numerically (finite-difference matrix diagonalization on a grid); (3) compare the first eight levels and plot the relative error against $n$.
- **Checks:** exact levels $0.668,\ 2.394,\ 4.697,\ 7.336$ (verify numerically); the WKB values $0.545,\ 2.364,\ 4.67,\ 7.31$; the error shrinks with $n$ (semiclassical accuracy improves toward large quantum numbers).

## Pitfalls

- Treating the gate as recognition rather than recall: if you can only *read* a derivation you have not passed it.
- Skipping the written synthesis in favor of the programming projects.
- Writing the essay as a list of correspondences without stating what is assumed.
