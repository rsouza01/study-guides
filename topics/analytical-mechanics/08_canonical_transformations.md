# Module 08: Canonical transformations

**Books:** Goldstein 9.1 to 9.2; Landau and Lifshitz *Mechanics* sections 45 to 46 (optional); José and Saletan chapter 5 (canonical transformations as symplectomorphisms, optional).
**Time:** 2 weeks (four sessions).

## Why this matters for the bridge

A canonical transformation is a change of coordinates in phase space that **preserves the form of Hamilton's equations**. The four generating-function types look like bookkeeping. What matters is that $F_2(q,P)=\sum q_iP_i$ generates the *identity*, and adding a small term to it generates an *infinitesimal* transformation. That is the payload of Module 10 and the place where the whole structure turns: the infinitesimal generator of a symmetry is the quantity whose bracket becomes a commutator in quantum mechanics.

## Skip-test (40 minutes, no notes)

1. State the condition for a transformation $(q,p)\to(Q,P)$ to be canonical (the new variables obey Hamilton's equations with a new $K(Q,P,t)$) and the relation $\sum p\,dq-\sum P\,dQ=dF$ (for the time-independent case).
2. Write the four generating functions $F_1(q,Q,t)$, $F_2(q,P,t)$, $F_3(p,Q,t)$, $F_4(p,P,t)$ with their relations $p=\partial F_1/\partial q$, $P=-\partial F_1/\partial Q$, $p=\partial F_2/\partial q$, $Q=\partial F_2/\partial P$, and $K=H+\partial F/\partial t$.
3. Show $F_2=\sum q_iP_i$ generates the identity and $F_1=\sum q_iQ_i$ interchanges $q$ and $p$.
4. Verify that $q=\sqrt{\frac{2P}{m\omega}}\sin Q$, $p=\sqrt{2m\omega P}\cos Q$ is canonical and turns the oscillator Hamiltonian into $K=\omega P$.
5. Explain why not every canonical transformation has all four generating functions.

## Detailed topics (checklist)

**The idea (9.1) [Must]**
- Coordinate transformations $Q_i(q,p,t)$, $P_i(q,p,t)$; condition for canonicity from the variational principle: $\lambda(\sum p\dot q-H)-(\sum P\dot Q-K)=\frac{dF}{dt}$ with $\lambda=1$ (restricted canonical transformations); the generating function $F$ and its four types, connected by Legendre transformations.
- Time-independent versus time-dependent generating function: $K=H+\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}$.
- Point transformations $F_2=\sum f_i(q,t)P_i$ (change of generalized coordinates is a special canonical transformation; momenta transform accordingly).

**Examples (9.2) [Must]**
- The identity; the interchange $Q=p$, $P=-q$ (coordinates and momenta have no intrinsic meaning in phase space); the harmonic oscillator to $(Q,P)$ with $K=\omega P$ ($Q$ cyclic, $P=E/\omega$ constant); the displacement $F_2=\sum q_iP_i+\epsilon G$ (preview).
- Time-dependent examples: translation $q\to q-vt$.

**Extended canonical transformations [Could]**
- Scalings $Q=\alpha q$, $P=\beta p$ with $\alpha\beta=\lambda\ne1$ and $K=\lambda H$ (the "extended" transformations).

**Group structure [Should]**
- The composition of canonical transformations is canonical; the inverse is canonical; they form a group (the infinite-dimensional symplectomorphism group).

## Derive from scratch (A sessions, book closed)

1. The generating-function relations for $F_1$ from the variational principle.
2. The Legendre transformations among $F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4$.
3. $K=H+\partial F/\partial t$.
4. The oscillator transformation and $K=\omega P$.
5. The infinitesimal transformation generated by $F_2=\sum q_iP_i+\epsilon G(q,P)$.

## Bare-minimum exercises (with expected results)

1. **Oscillator transformation:** verify $\frac{\partial(q,p)}{\partial(Q,P)}=1$ (the transformation preserves areas); equivalently $\{q,p\}_{Q,P}=1$.
2. **Generating function of the oscillator:** show $F_1(q,Q)=\frac12m\omega q^2\cot Q$ gives $p=m\omega q\cot Q$, $P=\frac{m\omega q^2}{2\sin^2Q}$, and hence $q=\sqrt{\frac{2P}{m\omega}}\sin Q$, $p=\sqrt{2m\omega P}\cos Q$; $K=\omega P$ so $Q=\omega t+\beta$.
3. **Four types, one transformation:** for the identity transformation find $F_2$ and show $F_1$ does not exist; for $Q=p$, $P=-q$ find $F_1=qQ$ and show that $F_2$ does not exist as a function of $(q,P)$ (since $P=-q$ ties them). Not every canonical transformation has every type.
4. **Point transformation:** take the old coordinates $(r,\theta)$ and the new momenta $(P_x,P_y)$ with $F_2(r,\theta;P_x,P_y)=P_x\,r\cos\theta+P_y\,r\sin\theta$. Show $Q_x=r\cos\theta$, $Q_y=r\sin\theta$ (the new coordinates are the Cartesian ones) and $p_r=P_x\cos\theta+P_y\sin\theta$, $p_\theta=-P_xr\sin\theta+P_yr\cos\theta$.
5. **Classic check:** show $Q=\ln\left(1+\sqrt q\cos p\right)$, $P=2\left(1+\sqrt q\cos p\right)\sqrt q\sin p$ is canonical by computing $\{Q,P\}_{q,p}=1$.
6. **Time-dependent:** for $Q=q-vt$, $P=p-mv$ find $F_2(q,P,t)$ and $K$; for $H=\frac{p^2}{2m}$ show $K=\frac{P^2}{2m}$ (free particle in a moving frame: Galilean transformation).
7. **Form preservation:** show by direct differentiation that for a canonical transformation $\dot Q=\partial K/\partial P$ and $\dot P=-\partial K/\partial Q$.
8. **Infinitesimal transformation:** with $F_2=qP+\epsilon G(q,P)$ show $Q=q+\epsilon\frac{\partial G}{\partial P}$, $p=P+\epsilon\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}$; to first order $\delta q=\epsilon\frac{\partial G}{\partial p}$, $\delta p=-\epsilon\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}$. For $G=H$ these are the equations of motion for an interval $\epsilon=dt$.
9. **Extended transformation:** for $Q=\alpha q$, $P=\beta p$ find the conditions on $\alpha,\beta$ for $K=\lambda H$ and interpret (scaling both variables rescales the symplectic form).
10. **Group property:** compose two canonical transformations (a translation followed by the interchange) and verify the result is canonical.

## Optional extension

Show that a transformation is canonical if and only if it preserves the symplectic area $\oint\sum p\,dq$ of every closed curve in phase space (Poincaré-Cartan integral invariant); this is the geometric content that Module 09 formalizes.

## Pitfalls

- Believing every canonical transformation has all four generating functions.
- Forgetting the $\partial F/\partial t$ term in $K$.
- Treating $Q,P$ as having a fixed physical meaning (position and momentum are interchangeable in phase space).
- Confusing a point transformation of coordinates with a general canonical transformation.

## Gate questions

1. What does it mean for a transformation to be canonical, in three equivalent ways (variational, symplectic area, preserved brackets)?
2. Why does $F_2=\sum q_iP_i$ generate the identity?
3. Why can phase-space coordinates and momenta be exchanged?
