**Time:** 2 weeks (four sessions). **A written deliverable, not just reading.**

## Why this matters for the bridge

This module answers the first blur directly: *is $[\hat q,\hat p]=i\hbar$ derived or asserted?* The honest answer has three parts. Dirac's argument shows that, given the algebraic properties a quantum bracket must have, the commutator is *forced* to be $i\hbar$ times the classical bracket **for the quantities where the correspondence can be imposed**. It does not derive that correspondence for all observables: the Groenewold-van Hove theorem says it cannot hold for all of them. And the correspondence between generators and symmetries (Module 10) tells you *why* $\hat p$ is $-i\hbar\partial_x$ without any appeal to a wavefunction. Part of the blurriness is real: quantization is a guess constrained by the Lie-algebra structure, not a functor from classical to quantum mechanics. The arrow you may have been drawing, classical $\to$ quantum, points the wrong way: **the classical theory is a limit of the quantum one, not its parent.**

## Detailed topics (checklist)

**Dirac's argument [Must]**
- Assumptions: the quantum bracket $[A,B]$ (the commutator) is bilinear, antisymmetric, satisfies the Leibniz rule and the Jacobi identity, and reduces to something classical in the appropriate limit.
- The key step: expanding $[A_1A_2,B_1B_2]$ in two ways using the Leibniz rule (with $[\,,]$ the quantum Poisson bracket and $A_1B_1-B_1A_1$ the ordinary commutator) forces $A_1B_1-B_1A_1=\kappa\,[A_1,B_1]$ with a constant $\kappa$ independent of the variables; $\kappa=i\hbar$ (the $i$ because commutators of Hermitian operators are anti-Hermitian; the value of $\hbar$ is fixed by experiment). **Note carefully what is derived (the form of the bracket, given the properties and the limit) and what is assumed (the properties; that the correspondence holds at all).**
- The identification of generators: $\hat p$ generates translations because $\hat T(a)=e^{-ia\hat p/\hbar}$ and $\hat T^\dagger\hat x\hat T=\hat x+a$.

**The correspondence table [Must]** (write it from memory)

| Classical | Quantum |
| --- | --- |
| $\{q_i,p_j\}=\delta_{ij}$ | $[\hat q_i,\hat p_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}$ |
| $\dot u=\{u,H\}$ | $i\hbar\,\dot{\hat u}=[\hat u,\hat H]$ (Heisenberg) |
| $G$ generates a canonical transformation, $\delta u=\epsilon\{u,G\}$ | $\hat U=e^{-i\epsilon\hat G/\hbar}$, $\delta\hat u=\frac{\epsilon}{i\hbar}[\hat u,\hat G]$ |
| $\{L_i,L_j\}=\epsilon_{ijk}L_k$ | $[\hat L_i,\hat L_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat L_k$ |
| Liouville: phase volume preserved | Unitarity: norm preserved |
| $\{G,H\}=0\Rightarrow G$ conserved | $[\hat G,\hat H]=0\Rightarrow G$ conserved |
| Poisson-bracket Lie algebra | Operator Lie algebra; representations |

**Where it breaks: Groenewold-van Hove [Must]**
- Statement: there is no linear map $f\mapsto\hat f$ from *all* polynomial classical observables in $(q,p)$ to operators such that $\widehat{\{f,g\}}=\frac1{i\hbar}[\hat f,\hat g]$ for all $f,g$ and $\hat q$, $\hat p$ have their standard form. Ordering ambiguities ($qp$ versus $\frac12(\hat q\hat p+\hat p\hat q)$) are not a notational nuisance but the visible symptom of an obstruction.
- What it does *not* say: that quantum mechanics is inconsistent. It says that canonical quantization is a heuristic that works on a restricted class of observables (for example polynomials of degree at most two in $q,p$ via Weyl symmetric ordering, which map consistently into the oscillator-type algebra).
- Related ideas (awareness only): Weyl quantization, the Moyal bracket (a deformation of the Poisson bracket in powers of $\hbar$), Stone-von Neumann (uniqueness of the representation of the canonical commutation relations).

**Symmetries versus observables [Should]**
- Minimal coupling changes brackets: the kinetic momentum $\boldsymbol\pi=\mathbf p-q\mathbf A$ satisfies $\{\pi_i,\pi_j\}=q\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ classically and $[\hat\pi_i,\hat\pi_j]=i\hbar q\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ quantum mechanically (this is the origin of Landau levels).

## Derive from scratch (A sessions, book closed)

1. Dirac's argument: from bilinearity, antisymmetry and Leibniz, derive $[A,B]=i\hbar\{A,B\}^\wedge$ up to a universal constant.
2. $\hat T^\dagger(a)\hat x\hat T(a)=\hat x+a$ from $[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar$.
3. The Heisenberg equation from $\hat u(t)=e^{i\hat Ht/\hbar}\hat u\,e^{-i\hat Ht/\hbar}$.
4. $[\hat L_i,\hat L_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat L_k$ from the canonical commutators.
5. The commutator of kinetic momenta in a magnetic field.

## Bare-minimum exercises (with expected results)

1. **Functions of $p$:** $[\hat x,f(\hat p)]=i\hbar f'(\hat p)$ and $[\hat p,g(\hat x)]=-i\hbar g'(\hat x)$; identical in form to the classical brackets with $\{\,,\}\to\frac1{i\hbar}[\,,]$.
2. **Translation:** $\hat T(a)=e^{-ia\hat p/\hbar}$ gives $\hat T^\dagger\hat x\hat T=\hat x+a$; compare with Module 10 exercise 1.
3. **Heisenberg oscillator:** for $\hat H=\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2\hat x^2$ the Heisenberg equations are $\dot{\hat x}=\hat p/m$, $\dot{\hat p}=-m\omega^2\hat x$, *identical* to the classical equations (a special property of quadratic Hamiltonians).
4. **Ehrenfest:** show $\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat x\rangle=\frac{\langle\hat p\rangle}m$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat p\rangle=-\langle V'(\hat x)\rangle$. For $V=\lambda x^4$ the difference between $\langle V'\rangle$ and $V'(\langle x\rangle)$ is the first quantum correction ($\propto$ the variance of $x$).
5. **Ordering:** $[\hat x^2,\hat p^2]=2i\hbar(\hat x\hat p+\hat p\hat x)$ while $\{x^2,p^2\}=4xp$; the quantum result equals $4i\hbar$ times the *symmetrically ordered* $xp$.
6. **Angular momentum:** from $[\hat x_i,\hat p_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}$ verify $[\hat L_i,\hat L_j]=i\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}\hat L_k$; line it up against Module 09 exercise 2.
7. **Minimal coupling:** show $\{\pi_i,\pi_j\}=q\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ classically and $[\hat\pi_i,\hat\pi_j]=i\hbar q\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$ quantum mechanically for $\boldsymbol\pi=\mathbf p-q\mathbf A$.
8. **A first look at the obstruction:** compute $[\hat x^3,\hat p^3]$ and compare with $i\hbar$ times the classical $\{x^3,p^3\}=9x^2p^2$. With the Weyl-ordered image $W(x^2p^2)=\frac14(\hat x^2\hat p^2+2\hat p\hat x^2\hat p+\hat p^2\hat x^2)$ one finds $[\hat x^3,\hat p^3]=9i\hbar\,W(x^2p^2)-\frac32i\hbar^3$: the quantum bracket of the images differs from the image of the classical bracket by an $O(\hbar^2)$ term (the Moyal correction). This is the elementary shadow of Groenewold's theorem: for cubic and higher observables the map cannot preserve brackets exactly.
9. **Unitarity versus Liouville:** show that $\hat U(t)=e^{-i\hat Ht/\hbar}$ preserves $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle$ and write the exact parallel statement for the flow $e^{t\{\cdot,H\}}$ preserving phase-space volume.
10. **Table from memory:** reproduce the correspondence table without notes and mark the rows where the correspondence is *exact* (quadratic observables) versus *asymptotic* in $\hbar$.

## Pitfalls

- Believing Dirac's argument derives $\hbar$ (it does not; $\hbar$ is fixed by experiment).
- Concluding from Groenewold-van Hove that quantization is inconsistent.
- Treating ordering ambiguities as a notational matter.
- Replacing $\{,\}$ by $\frac1{i\hbar}[,]$ for non-polynomial observables without checking.
